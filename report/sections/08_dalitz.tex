\section{Dalitz Plot Analysis}

\subsection{Charge-ordered Dalitz variables}

Three-body decays contain more kinematic information than a single reconstructed parent mass. The internal phase-space structure is studied with pairwise invariant masses,
\begin{equation}
  m_{ij}^2 =
  \left(E_i + E_j\right)^2
  -
  \left|\vec{p}_i + \vec{p}_j\right|^2 ,
\end{equation}
with each daughter assigned the kaon mass hypothesis and the squared masses reported in \gevfour{}.

The three tracks of a candidate carry labels $H_1$, $H_2$, $H_3$ in the ntuple, but these labels have no physical meaning: they are not ordered by charge. A $B^+ \to K^+K^+K^-$ candidate has two opposite-sign pairs, $K^+K^-$, and one same-sign pair, $K^+K^+$. Pair masses built directly from the labels, such as $m^2(H_1H_2)$, therefore mix same-sign and opposite-sign pairs from one candidate to the next, and do not define a fixed region of phase space. They are not usable for a comparison of $B^+$ and $B^-$.

The opposite-sign pairs are instead identified from the daughter charges, as the two pairs that contain the track whose charge is opposite to the total. Because the two same-sign kaons are identical particles, the plane is folded by ordering the two opposite-sign masses,
\begin{equation}
  m^2_{\mathrm{low}}(K^+K^-) \le m^2_{\mathrm{high}}(K^+K^-) .
\end{equation}
These coordinates do not depend on the track labelling, and they are the ones that map onto each other under CP: the $K^-K^+$ pairs of the conjugate $B^-$ decay. A $B^+$ and a $B^-$ candidate in the same bin then sit in the same region of phase space, which is what a bin-by-bin asymmetry requires. Both properties are covered by unit tests.

\subsection{Dalitz distribution}

A Dalitz plot displays the density of selected events in the plane of two pair-mass-squared variables. Resonant two-body substructures appear as bands, while interference and phase-space effects produce non-uniform densities. In a complete amplitude analysis these structures would be modelled with coherent sums of resonant and non-resonant amplitudes; the present analysis restricts itself to visualising the distribution.

Figure~\ref{fig:dalitz_density} shows all selected candidates, without a mass window; the local asymmetry study of Sec.~\ref{sec:local_dalitz} restricts itself to the signal region. The narrow $\phi(1020) \to K^+K^-$ band at $m^2_{\mathrm{low}} \approx 1.04~\gevfour$ is clearly visible, which is a check that the opposite-sign pairs are correctly identified: with label-based pairs the band is diluted across the three combinations.

Dalitz plots are especially relevant for CP-violation studies in multibody decays. Even if the integrated raw asymmetry is small, local asymmetries in restricted phase-space regions can be larger. This motivates a first bin-by-bin diagnostic of charge-dependent structures across the Dalitz plane.

\subsection{Local raw-asymmetry diagnostic}
\label{sec:local_dalitz}

The local diagnostic uses the candidates of the signal region, $5245 < m(hhh) < 5325~\mevcc$, which is the fitted peak position of Sec.~\ref{sec:mass_fit} widened by twice the fitted resolution. Restricting to that window keeps the diagnostic on the same candidates the mass fits describe; using every selected candidate instead would mix in the sidebands and the low-mass structure excluded from the fits. The window contains $1323$ candidates, $689$ of positive and $634$ of negative charge. Candidates are split into \Bplus{} and \Bminus{} samples using the total reconstructed daughter charge,
\begin{equation}
  q_{\mathrm{tot}} = q_1 + q_2 + q_3 .
\end{equation}
The $m^2_{\mathrm{low}}(K^+K^-)$ versus $m^2_{\mathrm{high}}(K^+K^-)$ plane is then divided into regular two-dimensional bins. In each bin $(i,j)$, the raw asymmetry is computed directly from candidate counts,
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{raw}}^{ij}
  =
  \frac{N_-^{ij} - N_+^{ij}}{N_-^{ij} + N_+^{ij}} ,
\end{equation}
where $N_+^{ij}$ and $N_-^{ij}$ are the numbers of \Bplus{} and \Bminus{} candidates in that Dalitz bin. The statistical uncertainty is estimated from binomial counting propagation, and a local significance is formed as $A_{\mathrm{raw}}^{ij}/\sigma(A_{\mathrm{raw}}^{ij})$.

The default study uses a $10\times10$ binning and masks bins with fewer than 20 candidates in the asymmetry and significance maps. The bin contents are still stored in the numerical output, but low-statistics regions are not interpreted visually. With this threshold, 23 out of 100 bins pass the requirement; about half of the grid lies outside the folded kinematic region and is empty by construction. The direct counting asymmetry over the whole window is
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{raw}} = -0.0416 \pm 0.0275 ,
\end{equation}
consistent with the fit-based value of Eq.~\ref{eq:raw_asymmetry_fit}, as it should be: the two use the same candidates, one subtracting the fitted background and the other not. The counting value does not correct for detector efficiency, phase-space acceptance, bin migration, production asymmetry, or detector-induced charge asymmetries.

The largest local significance over the 23 populated bins is $2.3$. Treating the bins as independent and each significance as a unit Gaussian under the no-asymmetry hypothesis, the probability that at least one of 23 bins fluctuates that far is
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{global}} = 1 - \left(1 - p_{\mathrm{local}}\right)^{23} = 0.40 ,
\end{equation}
so this diagnostic shows no evidence of local structure --- the expected outcome at this sample size. The published LHCb study of local asymmetries in this channel uses a far larger dataset and a finer binning of the Dalitz plane~\cite{lhcb3body}.

Figure~\ref{fig:local_counts_map} shows the selected-candidate population used to define the local bins. The occupancy is highly non-uniform, reflecting both phase-space structure and the effect of the selection. This count map is essential context for interpreting the local asymmetry and significance maps.

A proper local CP-asymmetry study would require signal-yield extraction or background subtraction in each Dalitz region, efficiency and acceptance corrections, treatment of migration between bins, and detector- and production-asymmetry corrections. The maps shown here should therefore be read as a conservative exploratory diagnostic rather than as evidence for a physical CP asymmetry.
