\section{Dalitz Plot Analysis}

\subsection{Pairwise invariant masses}

Three-body decays contain more kinematic information than a single reconstructed parent mass. The internal phase-space structure can be studied using pairwise invariant masses. For three daughter tracks labelled $K_1$, $K_2$, and $K_3$, the Dalitz variables used in this project are
\begin{align}
  m^2(K_1K_2), \qquad
  m^2(K_2K_3), \qquad
  m^2(K_1K_3).
\end{align}
Only two of these variables are independent for a fixed parent mass, but plotting different pairs is useful for visual inspection and for checking symmetries or structures involving different track orderings.

The pair mass is computed by summing the two daughter four-vectors:
\begin{equation}
  m_{ij}^2 =
  \left(E_i + E_j\right)^2
  -
  \left|\vec{p}_i + \vec{p}_j\right|^2 .
\end{equation}
As in the B-mass reconstruction, each daughter is assigned the kaon mass hypothesis. The resulting squared masses are reported in \gevfour{} to keep the numerical axis scales readable.

\subsection{Dalitz distributions and resonance structure}

A Dalitz plot displays the density of selected events in a two-dimensional plane of pairwise invariant-mass-squared variables. Resonant two-body substructures can appear as bands, while interference and phase-space effects can produce non-uniform densities. In a complete amplitude analysis, these structures would be modelled with coherent sums of resonant and non-resonant amplitudes. The present analysis restricts itself to visualising the distributions.

Dalitz plots are especially relevant for CP-violation studies in multibody decays. Even if the integrated raw asymmetry is small, local asymmetries in restricted phase-space regions can be larger. This motivates a first bin-by-bin diagnostic of charge-dependent structures across the Dalitz plane.

\subsection{Local raw-asymmetry diagnostic}

The local diagnostic uses the same selected candidates as the integrated raw-asymmetry study. Candidates are split into \Bplus{} and \Bminus{} samples using the total reconstructed daughter charge,
\begin{equation}
  q_{\mathrm{tot}} = q_1 + q_2 + q_3 .
\end{equation}
The $m^2(K_1K_2)$ versus $m^2(K_2K_3)$ plane is then divided into regular two-dimensional bins. In each bin $(i,j)$, the raw asymmetry is computed directly from candidate counts,
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{raw}}^{ij}
  =
  \frac{N_-^{ij} - N_+^{ij}}{N_-^{ij} + N_+^{ij}} ,
\end{equation}
where $N_+^{ij}$ and $N_-^{ij}$ are the numbers of \Bplus{} and \Bminus{} candidates in that Dalitz bin. The statistical uncertainty is estimated from binomial counting propagation, and a local significance is formed as $A_{\mathrm{raw}}^{ij}/\sigma(A_{\mathrm{raw}}^{ij})$.

The default study uses a $10\times10$ binning and masks bins with fewer than 20 selected candidates in the asymmetry and significance maps. The bin contents are still stored in the numerical output, but low-statistics regions are not interpreted visually. With this threshold, 26 out of 100 bins pass the requirement. The direct counting asymmetry over the selected candidates entering the local Dalitz study is
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{raw}} = -0.0748 \pm 0.0246 .
\end{equation}
This number differs from the fit-based integrated asymmetry quoted in the CP-asymmetry section because it is a direct candidate-counting diagnostic. It does not subtract fitted background and does not correct for detector efficiency, phase-space acceptance, bin migration, production asymmetry, or detector-induced charge asymmetries.

Figure~\ref{fig:local_counts_map} shows the selected-candidate population used to define the local bins. The occupancy is highly non-uniform, reflecting both phase-space structure and the effect of the selection. This count map is essential context for interpreting the local asymmetry and significance maps.

A proper local CP-asymmetry study would require signal-yield extraction or background subtraction in each Dalitz region, efficiency and acceptance corrections, treatment of migration between bins, and detector- and production-asymmetry corrections. The maps shown here should therefore be read as a conservative exploratory diagnostic rather than as evidence for a physical CP asymmetry.
