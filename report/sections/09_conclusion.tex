\section{Summary and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This project implements a complete introductory HEP analysis chain on LHCb CERN
Open Data. Starting from ROOT ntuples, the analysis reads selected branches with
\texttt{uproot}, processes candidates with \texttt{pandas} and \texttt{numpy},
reconstructs invariant masses, applies physics-motivated selections, fits the
mass spectrum by binned maximum likelihood, separates the charge-conjugate
samples, validates the statistical procedure with pseudo-experiments, evaluates
two categories of systematic uncertainty, and examines the three-body phase space
with Dalitz-plot distributions and a local asymmetry diagnostic.

The invariant-mass reconstruction shows a clear $B$-candidate peak after the
baseline selection, at a position consistent with the known $B^\pm$
mass~\cite{pdg}. The charge-separated fits give a raw asymmetry compatible with
zero within the quoted uncertainties, and the pseudo-experiments show that the
statistical procedure and its uncertainty are correctly calibrated. Two results
of this report are negative ones, and they are stated as such: the sideband
Random Forest does not separate signal from background on this sample, and the
double-Gaussian signal shape cannot be constrained by it.

The analysis remains intentionally simplified and does not constitute a precision
flavour-physics measurement. Several improvements would be required for a full
experimental analysis:
\begin{itemize}
  \item model the partially reconstructed and misidentified contributions below
        $5200~\mevcc$ explicitly, instead of excluding that region, which is the
        largest source of systematic uncertainty here;
  \item combine the MagnetUp and MagnetDown samples to cancel charge-dependent
        detector effects, and use a control channel to remove production and
        detection asymmetries;
  \item include detector effects, PID calibration, trigger efficiencies, and
        reconstruction asymmetries, validated on simulation and control samples;
  \item implement signal-shape alternatives such as a Crystal Ball function, on a
        sample large enough to constrain them;
  \item train the classifier before the tight preselection, with
        cross-validation and overtraining checks, if it is to be useful at all;
  \item perform the local Dalitz asymmetry study with per-bin background
        subtraction and efficiency corrections.
\end{itemize}

Despite these limitations, the project reproduces the structure of a real
experimental analysis: transparent event reconstruction, documented selection
choices, modular code, reproducible figures, signal extraction by likelihood fit,
uncertainty propagation, validation on pseudo-data, explicit and pre-registered
acceptance criteria for systematic variations, and a clear separation between
what has been measured and what would still be needed to claim a physics result.
