\section{Summary and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This project implements a complete introductory HEP analysis chain on LHCb CERN
Open Data. Starting from ROOT ntuples, the analysis reads selected branches with
\texttt{uproot}, processes candidates with \texttt{pandas} and \texttt{numpy},
reconstructs invariant masses, applies physics-motivated selections, fits the
mass spectrum by binned maximum likelihood, fits the charge-conjugate samples of
both magnet polarities simultaneously, validates the statistical procedure with
pseudo-experiments, evaluates a joint model and fit-range systematic, tests the
charge dependence of the mass shape, and examines the three-body phase space
with Dalitz-plot distributions and a local asymmetry diagnostic.

The invariant-mass reconstruction shows a clear $B$-candidate peak after the
baseline selection, at a position consistent with the known $B^\pm$
mass~\cite{pdg}. The simultaneous charge fits give a raw asymmetry compatible with
zero within the quoted uncertainties, consistent between the two magnet
polarities, and the pseudo-experiments show that the statistical procedure and its
uncertainty are correctly calibrated. Two results of this report are negative
ones, and they are stated as such: the sideband Random Forest does not separate
signal from background on this sample, and the double-Gaussian signal shape
cannot be constrained by it.

A third finding concerns the method. An excess on the low-mass flank of the
MagnetDown \Bplus{} peak, a fluctuation of about two standard deviations that the
independent MagnetUp sample does not reproduce, was absorbed by the \Bplus{}
background when the two charges were fitted separately. That made the asymmetry
depend on the fit range and the fit model, and it dominated the uncertainty
budget. Fitting the charges simultaneously with shared shapes, and averaging the
two polarities, removes that sensitivity: the systematic falls below the
statistical uncertainty.

The analysis remains intentionally simplified and does not constitute a precision
flavour-physics measurement. Several improvements would be required for a full
experimental analysis:
\begin{itemize}
  \item use a control channel to remove the production asymmetry and the kaon
        detection asymmetry that the polarity average does not cancel;
  \item calibrate the momentum scale separately for each charge and polarity, for
        which the opposite peak-position shifts of the two polarities give a hint;
  \item model the partially reconstructed contributions below $5200~\mevcc$
        explicitly, with a shape constrained by simulation, since the ntuple
        boundary at $5050~\mevcc$ leaves too little of it to constrain from data;
  \item include detector effects, PID calibration, trigger efficiencies, and
        reconstruction asymmetries, validated on simulation and control samples;
  \item implement signal-shape alternatives such as a Crystal Ball function, on a
        sample large enough to constrain them;
  \item train the classifier before the tight preselection, with
        cross-validation and overtraining checks, if it is to be useful at all;
  \item perform the local Dalitz asymmetry study with per-bin background
        subtraction and efficiency corrections.
\end{itemize}

Despite these limitations, the project reproduces the structure of a real
experimental analysis: transparent event reconstruction, documented selection
choices, modular code, reproducible figures, signal extraction by likelihood fit,
uncertainty propagation, validation on pseudo-data, explicit and pre-registered
acceptance criteria for systematic variations, and a clear separation between
what has been measured and what would still be needed to claim a physics result.
