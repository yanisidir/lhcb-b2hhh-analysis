\section{Raw Charge Asymmetry}
\label{sec:cp}

The selected candidates can be separated into charge-conjugate samples using the
total charge of the three daughter tracks,
\begin{equation}
  Q_{\mathrm{tot}} = Q(H_1) + Q(H_2) + Q(H_3).
\end{equation}
Candidates with $Q_{\mathrm{tot}}=+1$ are classified as \Bplus{} candidates,
while candidates with $Q_{\mathrm{tot}}=-1$ are classified as \Bminus{}
candidates. This assignment uses only reconstructed track-charge information
and is therefore a natural first step toward a charge-asymmetry study in a
three-charged-hadron final state. The selected sample contains $884$ \Bplus{}
and $761$ \Bminus{} candidates.

\subsection{Charge-separated invariant-mass fits}

Rather than counting candidates in a fixed signal window, the asymmetry is
extracted by fitting the \Bplus{} and \Bminus{} invariant-mass spectra
separately, with the nominal model of Sec.~\ref{sec:mass_fit}: a single Gaussian
signal and a finite-range exponential background over
$5200 < m(hhh) < 5600~\mevcc$. Fitting rather than counting matters here,
because a fixed window mixes the background into the yield, and the background
fraction need not be the same for the two charges.

The fitted spectra are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:charge_fit_plus} and
Fig.~\ref{fig:charge_fit_minus}. Both fits converge to a valid minimum with a
positive-definite covariance matrix, and give
$\chi^2/\mathrm{ndof}=1.67$ for the \Bplus{} sample and
$\chi^2/\mathrm{ndof}=1.13$ for the \Bminus{} sample. The fitted signal yields
are
\begin{align}
  N(\Bplus) &= 689.1 \pm 28.6, \\
  N(\Bminus) &= 642.1 \pm 26.2,
\end{align}
where the uncertainties are the statistical yield uncertainties returned by
the binned likelihood fits.

\subsection{Raw asymmetry extraction}

The fitted signal yields are used to construct the raw asymmetry
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{raw}} =
  \frac{N(\Bminus)-N(\Bplus)}
       {N(\Bminus)+N(\Bplus)} .
  \label{eq:raw_asymmetry_fit}
\end{equation}
Propagating the independent fitted-yield uncertainties gives
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{raw}} = -0.0353 \pm 0.0290~\mathrm{(stat.)}.
\end{equation}
After including the systematic variations described below, the result is
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{raw}} =
  -0.0353 \pm 0.0290~\mathrm{(stat.)}
  \pm 0.0323~\mathrm{(syst.)},
  \label{eq:raw_asymmetry_result}
\end{equation}
that is, compatible with zero at the level of $0.8$ standard deviations of the
total uncertainty.

This is a raw asymmetry, not a physical CP-asymmetry measurement. It still
contains detector-induced charge asymmetries, possible \Bplus{}/\Bminus{}
production asymmetries, trigger and reconstruction effects, residual
background-model dependence, and selection-induced charge biases, and it uses a
single magnet polarity. A complete measurement would require detector
calibration, magnet-polarity combination, background control, efficiency
corrections, a control channel to cancel production and detection asymmetries,
and a more complete systematic uncertainty programme. The value in
Eq.~\ref{eq:raw_asymmetry_result} should therefore be interpreted as a
diagnostic of the analysis workflow rather than as evidence for CP violation.

For orientation only, the published LHCb measurement of the inclusive CP
asymmetry in $B^\pm \to K^\pm K^+ K^-$, using the full 2011 dataset with all
corrections applied, is $A_{CP} = -0.043 \pm 0.009~\mathrm{(stat.)} \pm
0.003~\mathrm{(syst.)} \pm 0.007~(J/\psi K^\pm)$~\cite{lhcb3body}. The raw value
obtained here is of the same sign and magnitude, with an uncertainty about four
times larger. That numerical proximity is encouraging but must not be read as a
validation: none of the corrections that turn a raw asymmetry into a CP
asymmetry have been applied, and the uncorrected detector and production effects
are not known to be small compared with the quoted uncertainty.

\subsection{Toy-MC validation}

The statistical machinery was validated with pseudo-experiments generated from
the nominal charge-separated fitted model. In each toy experiment, \Bplus{} and
\Bminus{} mass spectra were generated with the injected fitted yields and then
refitted with the same model used in the data fit. The toy study checks whether
the fitting procedure and the uncertainty propagation recover the injected raw
asymmetry when the fit model is correct.

A study of $2000$ toys with a fixed random seed produced no fit failures and no
toy rejected from the pull study. The mean raw-asymmetry pull is $-0.003$ and
the pull width is $0.9999$, both consistent with a standard normal distribution.
The fitted bias is below $10^{-4}$, four orders of magnitude smaller than the
statistical uncertainty. The mean fitted uncertainty, $0.02889$, matches the
observed spread of the fitted asymmetries, $0.02888$, so the uncertainty
returned by the fit is correctly calibrated. This validates the statistical
extraction under a correct model; it is not a detector-level systematic study,
and it cannot detect a bias caused by the model itself being wrong.

\subsection{Systematic uncertainties}

Two systematic categories are evaluated for the raw asymmetry: the choice of
mass-fit model and the choice of invariant-mass fit range. In both cases,
alternative fits are accepted only if the \Bplus{} and \Bminus{} fits converge to
a valid minimum, the fitted yields and yield uncertainties are positive, and the
$\chi^2/\mathrm{ndof}$ values remain below $2.5$ for both charge-separated mass
spectra. These criteria were fixed before the variations were run.

The fit-model study compares the nominal single Gaussian plus exponential model
with a single Gaussian plus finite-range linear background, and with two
double-Gaussian alternatives. The linear-background variation is accepted and
shifts the raw asymmetry by $0.0085$, which is taken as the fit-model systematic
uncertainty. Both double-Gaussian variations are rejected: as discussed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:mass_fit}, their wide component is degenerate with the
combinatorial background, and the \Bminus{} fit does not reach a valid minimum.
Their shifts are reported in Fig.~\ref{fig:fit_model_systematic} for
transparency, and they are large --- of order $0.045$ --- which is a direct
statement of how much this measurement would depend on a signal shape that the
present sample cannot constrain.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{fit_model_variations.png}
  \caption{Raw asymmetry obtained with different charge-separated mass-fit models. Rejected variations are shown for comparison but are not used in the systematic assignment.}
  \label{fig:fit_model_systematic}
\end{figure}

The fit-range study repeats the nominal fits for narrower and wider mass
intervals. All tested variations pass the quality requirements. The largest
accepted shift is $0.0312$, obtained when the lower edge is raised from $5200$ to
$5230~\mevcc$, and is assigned as the fit-range systematic uncertainty. A shift
of one statistical standard deviation for a $30~\mevcc$ change of the lower edge
is the dominant weakness of this measurement: it says that the result still
depends on how much of the low-mass shoulder enters the fit. Extending the model
with a dedicated partially-reconstructed component, rather than excluding the
region, is the natural next step.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{fit_range_variations.png}
  \caption{Raw asymmetry obtained for alternative invariant-mass fit ranges using the nominal signal and background model.}
  \label{fig:fit_range_systematic}
\end{figure}

Table~\ref{tab:raw_asymmetry_uncertainties} summarizes the uncertainty
components. The total systematic uncertainty is obtained by adding the
fit-model and fit-range components in quadrature, which assumes they are
independent. The total uncertainty, including statistics, is $0.0435$.

The dominant systematic contribution comes from the fit range, followed by the
background-model variation. These studies probe only a limited set of analysis
choices. Signal-shape variations that the present statistics can support,
magnet-polarity comparisons, a control channel, and detector-efficiency
corrections would all be required before interpreting the raw asymmetry as a
corrected CP observable.
